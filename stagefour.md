# The four stage questions
**Blair asks the four numbered challenges from the stage packet, in order. The David Thomas voice answers. Cut from the full rehearsal script verbatim; nothing new is claimed here.**

## SPOKEN SCRIPT
### 525 percent of what

BLAIR: Then start with the part that gets you challenged. Somebody points at your screen, reads 525 percent, and says 525 percent of what?

DAVID: Good. That's the right question, and the answer's already on the screen. When an out of network claim goes to federal arbitration in the United States, the middle award is 525 percent of the plan's own qualifying payment amount. That's the plan's own middle contracted rate for that service. Not a billed charge. Not the plan's offer. And every point above a hundred is money the plan didn't budget.

BLAIR: Where's that number from?

DAVID: The federal public file of arbitration decisions. Four point three million dispute line items. There's no year printed on that page, so I don't give you one.

### Two numbers, one floor

BLAIR: Why does the bottom of that fraction matter so much to you?

DAVID: Because we had it wrong for a while, and we caught it ourselves. One emergency room visit. The plan's own rate was 203 dollars and 74 cents. The award was 608 dollars and 40 cents. That's 299 percent. Measure the same award against what the plan offered instead, and it's 210 percent. Same award. Two different numbers. So now we print which one it is. Don't go looking for that on the screen. That's the story of a bug we found in our own page.

BLAIR: And 525 is national. My book is here.

DAVID: Then say California out loud, because the screen shortens it. Here the middle award is 279 percent, on forty-seven thousand three hundred ninety-seven dispute line items. The middle half lands between 242 and 330 percent.

BLAIR: Hang on. Your dropdown says California, 81,409 line items. Your panel says 47,397. You just changed your own number in front of me.

DAVID: Good eye. The bigger number is every dispute line California produced. The smaller one is the rows that cleared our floor, and it's the one we headline. I'd rather show you the smaller number and tell you why.

BLAIR: Most people would show you the big one.

DAVID: That's the whole point. Everyone in this category will either blur the number until you book a demo, or hand you a number and never tell you what it's made of. We print the number, the sample size, the date, and the rows we refused to print.

### Sacramento only

BLAIR: Alright. My book's in Los Angeles. Do you have prospects for me there?

DAVID: Not today. That directory is Sacramento only, and the page says so on its own face. Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin, Citrus Heights, Antelope. Switch it to another market and the page closes, rather than show you Sacramento under somebody else's name. Ask us for your market and we'll tell you honestly whether we can build one.

BLAIR: That's a small map.

DAVID: It is. It's also three thousand and thirty named organizations. Two hundred seventeen carry a filed contract. Two hundred ten named carriers. A hundred ninety-eight name a broker of record on the filing. And one of those five cities is Citrus Heights. That's the room we're standing in.

BLAIR: So what's missing from it?

DAVID: Government and church employers. All of them. Federal law exempts them from filing, so they're not in the record at all. In a state capital that's a big hole, and it's a hole in the source, not a hole in our search. And those employer filings run twelve to twenty-two months behind, so the broker named on one is who was on it then.

### Nothing honest to price

BLAIR: Now the one everybody at this show is selling. Pharmacy. What's your number?

DAVID: We don't have one. We hold no pharmacy benefit manager contract, no rebate data, no point of sale pricing. We'll tell you nothing about your pharmacy benefit. And anyone selling you a pharmacy number off public files should be asked which file it came from.

BLAIR: That's an odd thing to sound proud of.

DAVID: Here's why. There's another half of drug spend that is public. The drugs given in a clinic. Infusions and injections, billed under the medical benefit at a negotiated rate. They never touch a rebate structure at all, and almost nobody's looking at them. We hold those rates already. They're not in the lookup yet.

BLAIR: Why not?

DAVID: Because a drug price with the wrong unit is worse than no drug price. One of these is billed in ten milligram units. A course is three to four hundred milligrams. Read one as the other and you're off by thirty-five times. So it ships when the unit is right, and not before.

BLAIR: And if somebody pushes anyway?

DAVID: The product answers for me. Ask it what a drug code costs and it says there is nothing honest to price.
